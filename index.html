About this project

A source-led record of West Bengal OBC policy

This independent research bundle brings together publicly available notifications, statutes, public-hearing notices, policy reports, and selected court records concerning Other Backward Classes (OBCs) in West Bengal.

Purpose

Make the record easier to examine

The project is designed as a chronological research desk. It helps readers trace a claim from a timeline entry to a notification, a hearing notice, a policy source, or a court record without treating summaries as a substitute for the original document.

Scope

Policy, process, and litigation

The bundle covers the statutory framework, State OBC-list changes, reservation and roster records, the 2025–26 notification sequence, public hearings, and a growing litigation tracker. It does not claim to be a complete government archive.

Use with care

Research aid, not legal advice

Some records are text-extracted or copied from publicly accessible mirrors. Court status, operative directions, and notification validity should always be confirmed from the issuing authority or the relevant court.

What OBC Means

OBC is a constitutional-administrative category for socially and educationally backward classes. It is not a religion quota, and it is not limited to Hindu caste names.

Core idea

OBC is about backward classes

“Other Backward Classes” refers to communities or classes identified as socially and educationally backward and inadequately represented. In practice, state lists use caste, sub-caste, occupation, community, conversion history, and social location as evidence markers, but the legal category is backward class.

Common myth

“Muslims have no caste, so they cannot be OBC”

That is too simple. Islam as a faith does not prescribe caste, but South Asian Muslim society has long had socially ranked occupational and descent communities. Groups such as Jolah/Ansari-Momin, Fakir/Sain, Nashya-Sekh, Pahadia Muslim, Rayeen/Kunjra, Hajjam, and Chowduli appear in West Bengal OBC records because the class or community was treated as backward, not because reservation was given to all Muslims.

How to read the files

Community evidence still matters

The useful question is not whether a whole religion has caste. The useful question is whether a particular class has evidence of social and educational backwardness, inadequate representation, and proper process through the Commission, survey, hearing, or notification route. This page keeps those source trails visible.

Scientific Methods for Determining Backwardness

A fair OBC decision should be evidence-led: define the class, measure social and educational disadvantage, test representation in public institutions, publish the data trail, and periodically review whether the benefit is reaching the intended class.

Step 1 · Define the unit

Backwardness is class-specific

The correct unit is not an entire religion or a vague surname group. It is the specific caste, sub-caste, occupational community, converted community, or locally known class whose disadvantage can be measured. West Bengal records show this logic: entries such as Bhangi (Muslim), Sekh/Seikh, Kazi/Kaji/Quazi/Quaji (Muslim), and SBCW-165 Category-B serial 97 are document-specific, not blanket religious reservation.

Step 2 · Measure disadvantage

Use multiple indicators, not one slogan

The Mandal framework grouped indicators into social, educational, and economic heads and used a weighted score. That is the right spirit: no single number proves backwardness by itself. A robust method checks school completion, literacy, dropout, landholding, occupation, housing, income, social stigma, manual or hereditary work, and exclusion from public posts.

Step 3 · Publish representation

Reservation depends on inadequate representation

Backwardness is incomplete without representation data. Departments should publish class-wise and category-wise counts in recruitment, promotions where relevant, educational admissions, scholarships, hostels, certificate issuance, and pending vacancies. Without this, the public cannot test whether a class is under-represented or whether benefits are being captured by better-placed groups.

Is the class socially backward? Local social status, traditional occupation, endogamy, residential segregation, stigma, dependence on low-status work, field verification. Household survey plus public objections, local body verification, and Commission hearing notes. Relying only on political claims, pressure groups, or community labels without field evidence.
Is it educationally backward? Literacy, school completion, dropout, higher education participation, gender split, district-wise variation. Use economic indicators as supporting evidence, then apply creamy-layer exclusion to avoid elite capture. Treating poverty alone as OBC backwardness; poverty policy and backward-class policy overlap but are not identical.
Is representation inadequate? Class/category-wise share in government jobs, educational admissions, public bodies, certificates, applications, selections, and vacancies. Publish department-wise annual data with sanctioned posts, filled posts, applicant pool, selected candidates, and backlog. Declaring under-representation without publishing denominators or without separating applicants from selections.
Did the process allow correction? Public notice, hearing date, objection register, evidence submitted, Commission advice, reasoned order. Link every notification to hearing notices, survey records, advice reports, and final reasons. Fast notification without a visible evidence trail, which later becomes vulnerable in court and public debate.

Why Caste Census and Departmental Data Matter

A caste or class census is not useful because identity labels are magic. It is useful because policy needs denominators. Representation data is the numerator. Together they let the public ask whether reservation is proportionate, under-inclusive, over-inclusive, or captured.

Population denominator

If a class has no reliable population estimate, every percentage argument becomes guesswork. The 2012 panchayat survey documents in this bundle show the practical problem: officials were instructed to enumerate households, carry the backward-class list, publish draft lists, and process claims and objections. That kind of procedure is the beginning of auditable data.

Representation numerator

Departments should publish annual tables showing how many people from each OBC category and, where legally and administratively possible, each listed class are present in services and admissions. This is how one can distinguish genuine inadequate representation from a merely loud demand.

Outcome audit

Reservation should be checked against outcomes: who received certificates, who applied, who was selected, who remained excluded, which districts lagged, and whether the creamy layer is filtering better-off households. Good data protects deserving groups and exposes misuse.

Good Uses and Misuses of OBC Reservation

Good side

Correcting structural exclusion

When applied to classes with proven social and educational backwardness and inadequate representation, reservation opens access to jobs, education, hostels, scholarships, and public legitimacy. It can convert invisible occupational communities into recognized citizens with a measurable route into public institutions.

Good side

Forcing the state to measure society

A serious OBC process requires surveys, hearings, lists, objections, certificate rules, and roster data. That can improve governance beyond reservation itself because the state must publish who is excluded, where, and why.

Misuse

Elite capture and vote-bank listing

Misuse happens when powerful subgroups enter without evidence, when creamy-layer rules are weak, when politically useful communities are added quickly, or when no one publishes outcomes. This harms genuinely backward classes because the benefit pool is diluted and public trust falls.

What do we lack more? In this bundle, the largest gap is not the final notifications. It is the underlying data: class-wise population denominators, class-wise representation in departments and admissions, Commission advice reports, benchmark survey tables, hearing records, objection-disposal registers, and annual outcome audits. The next best improvement would be a public evidence matrix linking every listed class to population, education, occupation, representation, hearing, Commission advice, notification, and later review.

These are not West Bengal notification files, but they explain the policy architecture used to understand OBC identification, Muslim OBCs, affirmative action, and evidence requirements.

1980 · Mandal Commission

Criteria for identifying socially and educationally backward classes

The Mandal Commission is the baseline framework for OBC policy. Its relevant section groups backwardness indicators under social, educational, and economic heads, uses a weighted score, and treats 11 points out of 22 as the threshold in its survey method. It also notes that caste-based indicators needed a separate approach for non-Hindu backward communities.

  2. 13 Jul 1994

    Founding reservation order: 5% for Backward Classes, with a creamy-layer schedule

    Order No. 347-TW/EC, dated 13 July 1994 (Scheduled Castes & Tribes Welfare Department), made the first reservation of 5% of vacancies filled by direct recruitment in state government service, local/statutory bodies, government-majority corporations, universities, aided schools, and the public sector, in favour of Backward Classes notified under the West Bengal Commission for Backward Classes Act, 1993. Clause (b) attached a detailed exclusion Schedule (Categories I–VI: constitutional posts, Class I/II officers, armed forces, professional class, property owners, and an income/wealth test then set at Rs. 2.5 lakh/year) — an early version of what later came to be called the "creamy layer" test. This order was later amended to raise the percentage (see 6 Nov 1997) and was still being cited as the source of the creamy-layer schedule in certificates issued in 2001.

    Related: 346-TW/EC list of 13.7.1994, 374(71)-TW/EC certificate instructions, 1056-BCW/EC 7% increase.

1. Minimum fields for every document

  • Document number: notification/order/memo/case number exactly as printed.
  • Document type: notification, order, corrigendum, hearing notice, court order, report, act, roster, certificate guideline.
  • Status: current, withdrawn, superseded, legacy, missing, draft, or verification-needed.
  • Short summary: one sentence describing legal or documentary effect.
  • Source: local PDF path now, cloud URL later, plus source authority if known.

2. Evidence checks before marking complete

  • Does the PDF text match the notification number, date, and printed title?
  • Does it add, amend, withdraw, classify, or merely implement another document?
  • What earlier document does it cite, and what later document changes it?
  • For inclusion/categorisation: is there a Commission advice, survey, hearing, or benchmark record?
  • For court records: what operative direction affects the current list or reservation percentage?

3. Missing-entry statuses

  • Missing locally: known source exists, file not yet added.
  • Source not found: cited in another record, original PDF not located.
  • Partial: source found only as quote, mirror, OCR, or bundled page inside another PDF.
  • Needs OCR: scanned source exists but text is not searchable.
  • Verification needed: source exists but date/number/content conflict needs checking.
