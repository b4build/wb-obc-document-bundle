SYNOPSIS

The Petitioners are constrained to approach this Hon’ble Court under Article 32 of the Constitution against the Notifications dated 18.05.2026 (“Impugned Notifications”), issued by the Governor of West Bengal, and against the West Bengal Backward Classes (Reservation in Posts) (Amendment) Act, 2026 (“2026 Amendment Act”), enacted pursuant to Bill No. 11 of 2026 as passed by the West Bengal Legislative Assembly on 29.06.2026, insofar as the said Act gives legislative form and permanence to the exclusion effectuated by the Impugned Notifications in the OBC list in the State of West Bengal.

By way of the two notifications of even date, the Governor has (i) withdrawn, with immediate effect, notifications issued between 08.05.2025 and 03.06.2025 (“2025 Notifications”), which had placed 140 classes in the State OBC list with 17% reservation on the basis of a benchmark survey of backwardness conducted on the recommendation of the West Bengal Commission for Backward Classes (“Commission”), and (ii) declared that the State OBC list consists of only the 66 classes of OBCs, from before the year 2010, entitled to avail 7% reservation. The 2026 Amendment Act, introduced as Bill No. 11 of 2026 on 22.06.2026 and since passed and enacted, omits Schedule I of the West Bengal Backward Classes (Other than SC and ST) (Reservation of Vacancies in Services and Posts) Act, 2012 (“2012 Act”) which contained the 77 classes, re-numbers the existing Schedule II (the 66 pre 2010 classes) as the new Schedule I, omits Schedule III and Section 16 of the 2012 Act, and recasts Section 5 to make reservation percentages and sub-categorisation a matter of executive notification “in consultation with” the Commission. The cumulative effect of the Impugned Notifications, now sought to be entrenched through the 2026 Amendment Act, is to permanently foreclose the rights and OBC entitlements of 77 communities, including the Petitioners, without any statutory process, Commission consultation, empirical basis, or hearing.

The impugned action has been taken in the wake of the judgment dated 22.05.2024 passed by the Division Bench of the Hon’ble Calcutta High Court in Amal Chandra Das v. State of West Bengal 2024 SCC OnLine Cal 5389 (“2024 Judgment”). Between 2010 and 2012, the State of West Bengal had included 77 additional classes in the State OBC list through executive notifications, following community-specific surveys, public hearings, and empirical assessment of backwardness conducted by the Commission under the West Bengal Commission for Backward Classes Act, 1993 (“1993 Act”). These 77 classes were subsequently given legislative force under Schedule I of the 2012 Act, which sub-categorised them into OBC-A (More Backward) and OBC-B (Backward) with 17% reservation. Writ petitions challenging the inclusion of these 77 classes were filed before the Hon’ble Calcutta High Court between 2011 and 2013. Critically, none of the 77 communities were impleaded or heard in those proceedings. By its 2024 Judgment, the Division Bench (i) quashed the executive notifications classifying the 77 classes as OBCs, (ii) struck down the OBC-A/OBC-B sub-classification, and (iii) directed, by Direction (j), that the Backward Classes Welfare Department (“BCW Department”), in consultation with the Commission, place a report before the State Legislature with recommendations for inclusion or exclusion of classes from the State OBC list. The State of West Bengal and the Commission filed SLPs before this Hon’ble Court bearing SLP(C) Nos. 17751–17755 of 2024 challenging the 2024 Judgment. Those SLPs remain pending and have not been finally decided.

Pursuant to the Commission's recommendations and the 2025 Notifications, OBC/Caste certificates of members of the 77 classes, including the Petitioners, were revalidated and freshly reissued, and such certificates have since been, and continue to be, relied upon to avail reservation benefits in public employment and admissions. The Impugned Notifications and the 2026 Amendment Act, by extinguishing the basis of these certificates, now threaten the continued availment of such benefits by persons already exercising them.

The Petitioners are members of communities forming part of the said 77 classes, whose OBC status was conferred following empirical surveys, public hearings, and the Commission’s recommendations. The Impugned Notifications have extinguished this status with immediate and permanent effect, and the 2026 Amendment Act now purports to confer permanent legislative finality on the very same exclusion, threatening and depriving the Petitioners’ communities of access to reservations in public employment and public educational institutions to which they are constitutionally entitled as socially and educationally backward classes under Articles 15(4) and 16(4) of the Constitution. Neither the Impugned Notifications nor the 2026 Amendment Act is preceded by any fresh Commission report, survey, or recommendation concerning the 77 classes. Beyond the immediate loss of reservation benefits, the impugned action violates the Petitioners’ right to equality under Article 14, inasmuch as the State has acted arbitrarily, without empirical foundation, and in a manner directly inconsistent with its own conduct over the preceding year, and has further sought to use the legislative process to achieve, and place beyond judicial reach, what it could not lawfully achieve by executive notification alone. The exclusion of identifiable backward communities from the protective framework of reservation, without due process and without reasoned basis, constitutes a violation of their fundamental rights and warrants urgent intervention by this Hon’ble Court.

The Petitioners have separately intervened in the challenge to the 2024 Judgment before this Hon’ble Court by way of an Intervention Application bearing IA. No. ____ in SLP(C) No. 17751/2024. The Impugned Notifications dated 18.05.2026 and the 2026 Amendment Act are arbitrary and unsustainable inasmuch as

1.  Neither was preceded by any consultation with or recommendation from the Commission, in direct violation of Sections 9 and 11 of the 1993 Act, which make such consultation a precondition for any inclusion in or exclusion from the State OBC list.

2.  Neither is supported by the Commission report which Direction (j) of the 2024 Judgment expressly required to be placed before the State Legislature before the OBC list could be finalised.

3.  Both directly nullify the effect of this Hon’ble Court’s interim order dated 28.07.2025 in SLP(C) No. 17422 of 2025, under whose protection the 2025 Notifications and the 140-class framework stood revived and were in full legal operation at the very moment of their purported withdrawal.

4.  Neither discloses any survey data, Commission report, or finding that the 77 communities have ceased to be backward, contrary to the requirement of contemporaneous, empirical, data-based identification laid down in Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (1992) Supp (3) SCC 217 and reaffirmed in Jarnail Singh v. Lachhmi Narain Gupta (2018) 10 SCC 396, a requirement binding the Legislature no less than the Executive.

5.  The 2026 Amendment Act, enacted while SLP(C) Nos. 17751–17755 of 2024 and SLP(C) No. 17422 of 2025 remain pending and undecided before this Hon’ble Court amounts to a colourable use of the legislative process to render those proceedings infructuous.

6.  The cumulative effect is an arbitrary, irrational, and discriminatory denial of the constitutional protection guaranteed to the Petitioners’ communities under Articles 14, 15(4) and 16(4) of the Constitution.

Under these circumstances, the Petitioners are constrained to approach this Hon’ble Court under Article 32 of the Constitution seeking the quashing of the Impugned Notifications dated 18.05.2026, appropriate declaratory relief in relation to the 2026 Amendment Act, and restoration of the 2025 Notifications to full legal operation. Hence, the present writ petition.

GROUNDS

A. BECAUSE Sections 9 and 11 of the 1993 Act make consultation with and advice of the Commission a precondition for any inclusion in or exclusion from the State OBC list, and Section 9(2) provides that the Commission’s advice is ordinarily binding on the State Government. Neither the Impugned Notifications nor the 2026 Amendment Act was preceded by any consultation with the Commission concerning the 77 classes. This is not a procedural irregularity susceptible to cure but goes to the root of the exercise. The statutory mechanism for identifying backward classes exists precisely to ensure that such determinations are made on empirical, expert foundations and not by executive or legislative fiat. By bypassing this mechanism entirely, the State has acted without jurisdiction, and the Impugned Notifications, and the 2026 Amendment Act to the extent it gives effect to the same exclusion, are void ab initio.

B. BECAUSE Direction (j) of the 2024 Judgment expressly required the BCW Department, in consultation with the Commission, to place a report before the State Legislature with recommendations for inclusion or exclusion of classes. Neither the Impugned Notifications nor the 2026 Amendment Act was preceded by any such Commission report being placed before the Legislature. The State has, thus, acted not merely in defiance of its own statute but in direct violation of the judicial direction it claims to give effect to, whether by executive notification or by subsequent legislation. A party cannot selectively rely upon a judgment to justify State action while simultaneously ignoring the conditions and process that the judgment mandates.

C. BECAUSE the entire constitutional framework governing identification of Other Backward Classes, as elaborated in Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (1992) Supp (3) SCC 217, requires the determination of backwardness to be made on the basis of empirical data, not by the State acting on its own motion. The State of West Bengal, in recognition of this mandate, enacted the 1993 Act and constituted the Commission. Having created this institutional mechanism, the State cannot bypass it, whether through executive notification or through legislation, whenever its conclusions prove inconvenient. Both the Impugned Notifications and the 2026 Amendment Act unilaterally revise the OBC list, removing 77 communities, without reference to the Commission, survey data, or any expert assessment. The same is constitutionally impermissible.

D. BECAUSE by its order dated 28.07.2025 in SLP(C) No. 17422 of 2025, this Hon’ble Court stayed the Calcutta High Court’s order dated 17.06.2025 which had stayed the 2025 Notifications. As a consequence, the 2025 Notifications, placing 140 classes in the State OBC list with 17% reservation, stood revived and were in full legal operation under the express protection of this Hon’ble Court. The Impugned Notifications directly nullified the effect of this Hon’ble Court’s interim order, and the 2026 Amendment Act now seeks to achieve, through legislation, the very same nullification. No State authority, including the Governor or the State Legislature can be arbitrarily, whimsically and unmeritoriously permitted to efface, directly or indirectly, an order passed by this Hon’ble Court. The impugned action is accordingly an affront to the authority of this Hon’ble Court and cannot be permitted to stand.

E. BECAUSE pursuant to the Commission's recommendations and the 2025 Notifications, OBC/Caste certificates of members of the 77 classes, including the Petitioners, were revalidated and freshly issued reflecting their inclusion in the State OBC list, and members of these communities have since relied upon such certificates to secure appointments, admissions and other benefits under the reservation regime. The doctrine of legitimate expectation obligates the State, having conferred a status and permitted its exercise over an extended period, not to withdraw the same abruptly, without notice, hearing, or safeguard. The Impugned Notifications and the 2026 Amendment Act, by invalidating the foundation of such certificates violate the Petitioners' legitimate expectation and constitute a further breach of Article 14.

F. BECAUSE the Impugned Notifications and the 2026 Act create a constitutionally impermissible and untenable situation whereby persons from the 77 communities who have already secured employment, admission or other benefits on the strength of revalidated certificates issued pursuant to the 2025 Notifications now face the risk of cancellation, reversal, or exclusion from rosters, while identically placed members of the same communities are simultaneously denied the opportunity to avail such benefits. This exposes existing certificate holders to arbitrary administrative action, including cancellation of certificates and consequent termination or debarment.

G. BECAUSE by its order dated 28.07.2025, this Hon’ble Court, at least at the interim stage, declined to endorse the proposition that the State Executive was disabled from acting upon the Commission’s recommendations under the 1993 Act. The impugned action, by treating the State as permanently disabled from operating beyond the 66 pre-2010 classes, gives effect in statutory form to a proposition that this Hon’ble Court has expressly declined to affirm. The State cannot, by executive action or by subsequent legislation, restore a legal position that this Hon’ble Court has notionally set aside.

H. BECAUSE Between February 2025 and June 2025, the State of West Bengal, acting through the BCW Department and the Commission, commissioned comprehensive benchmark surveys through professional agencies (BAE&S and CRI), approved their engagement at the Commission’s 461^(st) Meeting, sought consultation on sub-classification by letter dated 17.04.2025, and issued notifications on Commission recommendations placing 140 classes in the State OBC list with 17% reservation. Such course of conduct proceeded on the premise that the OBC list was open, revisable, and capable of expansion. The impugned action, less than a year later, treats the pre-2010 66-class list as the only permissible baseline, without any explanation, without any finding that the 2025 exercise was invalid, and without any competing empirical data. Reversing course in this manner, without reason or record, is manifestly arbitrary and completely impermissible.

I. BECAUSE there can be no gainsaying that identification of backward classes must be grounded in contemporaneous empirical data. A community, once identified objectively as backward on the basis of survey and empirical assessment, can only be excluded from the OBC list upon a finding, supported by fresh data, that it has ceased to be backward or is now adequately represented in State services. Neither the Impugned Notifications nor the 2026 Amendment Act contains any such finding. The Impugned Notifications or the 2026 Amendment Act do not provide any survey data, Commission report, or any empirical basis whatsoever for concluding that the 77 communities are no longer backward. To remove communities from the OBC list, whether by bare executive order or by statute, in the absence of any such finding, is to act in direct violation of the constitutional framework governing backward class identification.

J. BECAUSE Of the 140 classes in the 2025 OBC list, 64 are pre-2010 communities that have been retained, while 76 newly surveyed communities, found to be backward on the same benchmark survey methodology, have been excluded. Neither the Impugned Notifications nor the 2026 Amendment Act draws any distinction between these communities on the basis of any empirical criterion. This constitutes differential treatment of similarly situated communities without rational basis, in violation of Article 14, and is rendered permanent by the 2026 Amendment Act’s renumbering of the Schedules.

L. BECAUSE the 2025 Notifications were issued by the Governor after following a prescribed statutory process i.e., surveys, meetings, followed by the recommendations. In this regard, it is submitted that where the State has acted upon empirical data to confer a benefit or status, it cannot withdraw that benefit or status without notice, without reasons, and without reference to the conditions under which the original determination was made. The absence of any reason is itself a ground of invalidity under Article 14 of the Constitution.

M. BECAUSE Articles 15(4) and 16(4) of the Constitution empower the State to make special provisions for the advancement of socially and educationally backward classes and for reservations in public employment in their favour, reflecting an affirmative constitutional obligation to ensure substantive equality. The Petitioners’ communities were included in the OBC list on the basis of empirical findings of their backwardness. The impugned action denies them the protection that Articles 15(4) and 16(4) are designed to afford, without any finding that their condition has changed. Exclusion of a community found backward by a competent authority, without any fresh assessment, amounts to a denial of constitutional protection that the State is obligated to extend.

N. BECAUSE as held in Indra Sawhney and reaffirmed in Jarnail Singh v. Lachhmi Narain Gupta (2018) 10 SCC 396, identification of backward classes must be subjected to periodic review, and the OBC list must at all times reflect empirical reality. The 2025 exercise was precisely such a periodic review, conducted in accordance with the High Court’s own methodology. The Impugned Notifications, and the 2026 Amendment Act, have preempted and nullified that review by reverting to a static pre-2010 list, in direct contradiction of the constitutional requirement that backward class identification remain a living, data-driven process.

O. BECAUSE the 2026 Amendment Act does not cure the absence of empirical foundation underlying the exclusion of the 77 classes, but in fact, re-enacts that exclusion in statutory form. Neither the Statement of Objects and Reasons nor any clause of the 2026 Amendment Act refers to any survey, report, or recommendation concerning the 77 classes. The Act proceeds by the bare device of omitting Schedule I of the 2012 Act and renumbering Schedule II as the new Schedule I. Legislative ratification of a conclusion that the State Executive reached impermissibly in the present case without Commission consultation or empirical basis reinforces the constitutional infirmity, and remains amenable to challenge under Articles 14, 15(4) and 16(4).

P. BECAUSE the 2026 Amendment Act was introduced and passed while SLP(C) Nos. 17751–17755 of 2024 (challenging the very 2024 Judgment invoked to justify the impugned action) and SLP(C) No. 17422 of 2025 remain pending and undecided before this Hon’ble Court. The State has whimsically and arbitrarily enacted a legislation that gives permanent and irreversible effect to the outcome of a judgment that is itself sub judice, on the very subject matter pending adjudication before this Hon’ble Court. The same is a colourable exercise calculated to render the pending proceedings infructuous, and is liable to be set aside as an impermissible interference with the judicial process.

Q. BECAUSE the scheme of the 1993 Act, including as recast by Section 5 of the 2026 Amendment Act, vests the substantive power of identification, inclusion and exclusion of classes from the OBC upon the recommendations of the Commission. The exclusion of the 77 classes, effected first by the Impugned Notifications and now sought to be entrenched through the omission of Schedule I by the 2026 Amendment Act, was at no stage preceded by any Commission recommendation for exclusion. The impugned action is accordingly against the very statutory scheme that it purports to operate under, and without legal authority.

R. BECAUSE the consequences of the impugned action are immediate and concrete, not abstract or prospective. Recruitment processes, public employment selections, and admissions to public educational institutions are being conducted on the basis of a 66-class OBC list that excludes the Petitioners’ communities. Every recruitment cycle conducted in reliance thereon permanently deprives members of these communities of seats and posts to which they would otherwise be entitled. The deprivation of reservation benefits for socially and educationally backward classes, caused by State action that is void, arbitrary and contrary to law, constitutes a continuing violation of the Petitioners’ fundamental rights under Articles 14, 15(4) and 16(4) of the Constitution, warranting urgent intervention by this Hon’ble Court under Article 32, regardless of the eventual fate of the 2024 Judgment in the pending SLPs.

QUESTIONS OF LAW

1. Whether the Impugned Notifications, and the 2026 Amendment Act in so far as it forecloses the OBC status of the 77 classes, are valid when issued/enacted without any consultation with or recommendation from the Commission as mandated by Sections 9 and 11 of the 1993 Act?

2. Whether enactment of the 2026 Amendment Act, without the Commission report contemplated by Direction (j) of the 2024 Judgment ever being placed before the Legislature, constitutes compliance with that direction, or is itself void as having been undertaken in breach of the very judgment it purports to implement?

3. Whether legislative ratification of an executive determination of backwardness, made without Commission consultation or empirical data is in violation of Articles 14, 15(4) and 16(4) of the Constitution?

4. Whether the 2026 Amendment Act, enacted while the 2024 Judgment remains sub judice before this Hon'ble Court in pending SLPs constitutes an impermissible interference with proceedings pending before this Hon'ble Court and an attempt to render such proceedings infructuous?

5. Whether the exclusion of the 77 classes, first by the Impugned Notifications and now by the 2026 Amendment Act, nullifies the effect of this Hon'ble Court's interim order dated 28.07.2025 in SLP(C) No. 17422 of 2025, and is for that reason liable to be set aside?

6. Whether withdrawal of OBC status from a community, by executive notification or subsequent legislation, without notice, without a finding of changed circumstances, and without opportunity to present community-specific data before any forum, violates Articles 14 and 21 of the Constitution?
